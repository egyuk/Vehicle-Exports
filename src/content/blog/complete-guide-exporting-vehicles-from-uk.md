## Why Export from the UK?

The United Kingdom is one of the world's largest exporters of used vehicles, and for good reason. British cars are well-maintained, regularly serviced, and subject to annual MOT inspections that ensure roadworthiness. This means that even a used vehicle from the UK is likely to be in better condition than comparable cars from many other markets.

## Step 1: Choose the Right Vehicle

Before anything else, you need to decide what type of vehicle suits your needs. Consider factors like:

- **Climate and terrain** — SUVs and pickups are popular in Africa and the Middle East, while sedans do well in the Caribbean.
- **Fuel availability** — Diesel is more readily available in some regions than others.
- **Import regulations** — Some countries restrict vehicles older than a certain age.
- **Left-hand vs right-hand drive** — The UK drives on the left, so vehicles are right-hand drive. Many African and Caribbean countries also drive on the left, making UK cars a natural fit.

## Step 2: Understand the Documentation

Every exported vehicle requires specific paperwork:

- **V5C Registration Certificate** — Proof of ownership
- **Purchase Invoice** — For customs valuation
- **Export Certificate** — Issued by the DVLA
- **Bill of Lading** — Shipping document
- **Pre-shipment Inspection Certificate** — Required by some countries

## Step 3: Choose Your Shipping Method

There are two main shipping options:

### RoRo (Roll-on Roll-off)
The vehicle is driven onto a cargo ship and secured for transport. This is the most affordable option for single vehicles.

### Container Shipping
The vehicle is loaded into a shipping container. More expensive, but offers better protection and allows you to ship personal items alongside the vehicle.

## Step 4: Handle Customs and Import Duties

Import duties vary significantly by country. Research your destination's requirements thoroughly. Common costs include:

- Import duty (typically 10-35% of vehicle value)
- VAT or sales tax
- Registration fees
- Inspection fees

## Step 5: Register Your Vehicle

Once the vehicle arrives and clears customs, you'll need to register it locally. Requirements vary, but typically include a roadworthiness inspection and proof of insurance.
